# Video media

<!-- moved out of the root CLAUDE.md by /doctor on 2026-10-13 -->

Path: `src/video/`

`media.ts` is the shared source-agnostic video engine (moved from `src/tiktok/`) — yt-dlp download (duration cap is **required**, no default: each vertical states its own, since the shared 600s one silently capped TikTok at 10 min), whisper transcription, ffmpeg keyframe extraction, plus the per-host URL helpers (`extractTikTokVideoId` — now host-gated so X's `/video/1` media-slot suffix can't misfire — `extractXStatusId`, `canonicalXStatusUrl`). Used by the TikTok and X-video verticals. The frame budget GROWS past 10 min (~40s spacing until `FRAME_BUDGET_MAX` = 60 binds at 40 min; past that spacing grows again — measured 62s/frame at 62 min, 180s at the X 3h cap) rather than staying flat at 30, which sampled a 62-min tutorial once per 124s. That ceiling is ONE shared constant: the same number as a second literal inside `extractKeyframes` is what made the first raise inert (budget 60 in, 30 frames out). `summarizeTimeoutFor` scales the summarize call with the frame count (600s floor + 24s per frame past 30 — the rate the floor itself implies). Beside it, `short-video.ts` is the ONE short-video capture job both verticals run (they were measured copy-paste twins), with every difference a field of `ShortVideoSpec` — cap, work dir, log category and noun, trace source, canonical url, ingest path, collection, and whether the tail warns about degraded frame Reads; `short-video-prompt.ts` (pure — the prompts page composes from it), `short-video-finish.ts` and `short-video-kinds.ts` are its prompt builders, post-model tail and kind set. **A caller URL reaches yt-dlp only through the route gate** (`src/dashboard/routes/url-gate.ts` — parse once, hand the `href` downstream, never the raw string; the TikTok and X routes add their host set and, for X, an anchored status path), plus the per-spec `ytDlpExtractors` → `--use-extractors` (X pins `twitter`, so a media-less tweet's link hand-off cannot reach `[generic]`; TikTok pins `tiktok` alone: its route resolves every short link (vm./vt., and `/t/<code>` on any host) itself, gating each redirect hop before requesting it, and answers 400/502 when that does not end on a video; it then rewrites the bare and `m.` hosts to `www.`, and the `/v/<id>.html`, `/embed/<id>`, `/embed/v2/<id>` and `/player/v1/<id>` paths to the id-only `/@/video/<id>` form `TikTokIE` matches (other embed-like paths stay unmapped: a short link landing on one is refused as `no_video`, and a pasted one fails at yt-dlp's `tiktok`-only allowlist) — `resolveTikTokShortLink` and `tiktokDownloadUrl` in `src/dashboard/routes/tiktok-url.ts`; yt-dlp is never handed a short link, because its `vm.tiktok` extractor would re-follow the chain ungated). An empty allowlist throws rather than dropping the flag, and every yt-dlp run, download and probe, passes `--ignore-config --no-plugin-dirs` (`YTDLP_ISOLATION_ARGS`), so neither a `yt-dlp.conf` nor a plugin extractor can widen the allowlist.

**Every media child runs as its own process group** (`PROC_GROUP` → `runProc`'s `processGroup`), so a timeout's signals reach the ffmpeg yt-dlp starts for a merge or fixup. A group leader is a session leader, so Ctrl-C on `bun run dev` and muninn's `shutdown()` no longer reach it on their own: `runProc` keeps a registry of live groups, `shutdown()` drains it first (`killLiveProcessGroups`, SIGKILL), and run-proc's `exit`/SIGINT/SIGTERM/SIGHUP hooks drain it on every other way out. A process with a listener of its own for the signal (`on` or `once`, registered before or after the first group) keeps its own exit, and the hook SIGKILLs the groups at once; the hook is prepended to the listener list for the `once` case. A process with no listener gets SIGTERM to every group, up to 1 s for them to go (`RUN_PROC_EXIT_TERM_GRACE_MS`), SIGKILL, and then the signal's default death re-raised; the `exit` hook does the same SIGTERM, wait and SIGKILL synchronously (on Linux the wait runs its full second, since an unreaped leader still counts). A signal the process inherited as ignored (`nohup` for SIGHUP, a background job of a non-interactive shell for SIGINT) gets no hook, so it stays ignored: `inheritedIgnore` reads `SigIgn` from `/proc/self/status` on Linux and `sigaction(2)` through `bun:ffi` on macOS. A timed-out call leaves no timer and no pipe read behind, so a process that left the group and holds a pipe does not keep the parent alive.
