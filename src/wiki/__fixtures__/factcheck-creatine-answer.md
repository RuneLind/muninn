Six of the eight claims hold up against the live web; one is contradicted by the pooled meta-analytic estimate and one overstates what the dosing evidence shows.

### ✅ Claim 1/8 — Creatine loading protocol dosage

The claimed protocol — 20 g/day split into four 5 g doses for 5–7 days, followed by 3–5 g/day maintenance — matches the widely cited standard creatine loading regimen (consistent with ISSN-based guidance), confirmed across independent sources.

Confidence: 80/100

Sources: [creatinepedia.com](https://creatinepedia.com/dosing/creatine-dosing-protocols-summary)

### ✅ Claim 2/8 — Creatine synthesis amino acids

Creatine is endogenously synthesized from three amino acids — arginine (contributing its amidino group), glycine (fully incorporated), and methionine (contributing a methyl group via SAMe) — via the AGAT/GAMT pathway, consistent with the peer-reviewed literature on creatine metabolism.

Confidence: 85/100

Sources: [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21387089/)

### ✅ Claim 3/8 — Creatine primary storage location

Creatine is overwhelmingly stored in skeletal muscle (commonly cited as ~95% of total body creatine, with the remainder in tissues like brain and heart), and the phosphocreatine/creatine kinase system acts as a rapid buffer to regenerate ATP during short, high-intensity bursts of effort.

Confidence: 80/100

Sources: [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4898252/)

### ❌ Claim 4/8 — Lean muscle mass gain from creatine in trials

A meta-analysis of older adults found creatine supplementation combined with resistance training increased lean tissue mass by a mean difference of **1.32 kg** (95% CI: 0.93–1.72) versus resistance training with placebo. Other meta-analyses report similar ~1.2–1.4 kg estimates, so "roughly 1kg" understates the pooled estimate.

Confidence: 85/100

Sources: [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8229907/)

### ✅ Claim 5/8 — Creatine supplementation raises serum creatinine

Serum creatinine commonly rises after starting creatine supplementation because creatine is metabolized into creatinine as a byproduct; this elevation reflects increased production, not impaired kidney function, and can falsely lower estimated GFR calculations.

Confidence: 85/100

Sources: [primarycarenotebook.com](https://primarycarenotebook.com/pages/general-information/creatine-supplements-and-raised-creatinine-levels)

### ✅ Claim 6/8 — Cystatin C unaffected by creatine supplementation

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (Gualano et al., 2008, *Eur J Appl Physiol*) found serum creatinine rose specifically in the creatine-supplemented group, while cystatin C declined at the same rate in both the creatine and placebo groups — i.e. no creatine-specific effect on cystatin C, consistent with it being a GFR marker insensitive to muscle creatine pool size.

Confidence: 82/100

Sources: [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11483778/)

### ⚠️ Claim 7/8 — Cognitive benefits strongest at maintenance doses

Evidence for this specific comparison is mixed. A 2025 narrative review (Frontiers in Nutrition) notes maintenance dosing (3–5 g/day) is "extensively endorsed" for cognitive benefit in older adults, but explicitly states observed cognitive effects "were not contingent upon dosage (approximately 2.2–20 g/day)" — i.e. no dosing strategy was shown superior for cognition.

Confidence: 45/100

Sources: [alzdiscovery.org](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/cognitive-vitality/ratings/creatine), [frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1687719/full)

### ✅ Claim 8/8 — Creatine monohydrate is the most-studied form

The Gatorade Sports Science Institute review on 25 years of creatine research explicitly states that creatine monohydrate "is the form of creatine that has been most studied, and therefore the greatest amount of efficacy and safety data are available for this form", and that no alternative formulation (e.g. HCl, buffered) has shown an advantage.

Confidence: 85/100

Sources: [gssiweb.org](https://www.gssiweb.org/sports-science-exchange/article/the-safety-and-efficacy-of-creatine-monohydrate-supplementation-what-we-have-learned-from-the-past-25-years-of-research)
